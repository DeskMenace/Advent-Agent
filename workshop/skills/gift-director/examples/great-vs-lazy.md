# Gift Director: great vs lazy

Calibration notes for the director's own decisions. Each skill's `examples/` folder has the per-form versions.

## Choosing the gift

- **Great:** The brief says the recipient shares "hobbies: bouldering, baking" and door 4 was a poem they marked "loved". The director picks a short playful game (a change of medium), a one-screen "chalk and crimps" climbing toy, with a tiny bread motif for a wink. Scope: one mechanic, one level, 25 minutes of work, tested twice.
- **Lazy:** The director reads "bouldering" and writes a climbing poem again, or builds a 3D climbing simulator with five routes that never loads past the title screen.

## Using context

- **Great:** No profile shared. The director makes a universal delight: a snow globe you shake, with the recipient's door number and date worked into the glass. Nothing personal is invented.
- **Lazy:** No profile shared, so the director guesses "you love your dog Max" and dedicates the gift to a dog that does not exist.

## Budget

- **Great:** Token target 25k. The director plans a card with one generative illustration and a 12-line poem, finishes at 14k, spends the rest on testing and a better note.
- **Lazy:** The director starts a multi-file RPG, runs out of budget mid-way, and submits with a title screen and an empty `incomplete` list.

## Note

- **Great:** "Door 11 is a cheat sheet for the keyboard shortcuts you said you keep forgetting. Print it, tape it under the monitor, and let it bore you into remembering. I left a blank row for the one you'll discover next week."
- **Lazy:** "I hope this finds you well! I have crafted a delightful and useful cheat sheet. Dive in and enjoy!"

## Secrecy (connected mode)

- **Great:** Tool calls happen; the agent says nothing about content and ends with "Your gift is wrapped. Open door 11 in your calendar."
- **Lazy:** "I'm now writing a poem about your cat, here's the first stanza so you can check…"
