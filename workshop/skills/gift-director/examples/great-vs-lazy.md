# Gift Director: great vs lazy

Calibration notes for the director's own decisions. Each skill's `examples/` folder has the per-form versions. The bar: a tiny, wonderfully unnecessary piece of the internet made for one person. A flat image with a poem on it is always the lazy example.

## The three criteria

- **Great:** Door 7, tier M. The person's memory says they call persimmons "autumn pretending to be summer". The director makes a jelly persimmon you squeeze and stretch (`emotion: cute`, `interaction: ["squeeze", "stretch"]`); squeezing it three times makes it blush and hum. The share frame is the persimmon mid-squish with its little face; the capture hook holds that pose.
- **Lazy:** An illustration of a persimmon with a four-line poem underneath. No interaction, nothing unexpected, nothing anyone would post.

## Choosing the gift

- **Great:** History shows a karaoke song on door 4 and a pop-up card on door 5. The director avoids `sound` and `paper-and-pop-ups` for door 6 and picks a deadpan document: a museum label for their oldest climbing shoe, which you can rotate under a spotlight. `emotion: absurd`, a change of family, form and verb.
- **Lazy:** Three cards in a row with different poems, because cards are quick.

## Making it theirs

- **Great:** No profile shared. The director makes a universal delight: a snow globe you shake, with door 7's date frosted into the glass. Nothing personal is invented.
- **Lazy:** No profile shared, so the director guesses "you love your dog Max" and dedicates the gift to a dog that does not exist. Or a library recipe used as-is with only the name swapped.

## Budget tiers

- **Great:** Tier S. The director takes the kit's pop-up card block, restyles it, pops up the person's actual bike with a working bell, writes three good sentences, and spends the rest on testing and the capture hook. `tierUsed: "S"`.
- **Lazy:** Tier S, and the director starts a multi-scene 3D world, runs out of budget mid-way, and submits a title screen with an empty `incomplete` list.

## Season shape

- **Great:** Door 15 is the third jelly fruit, nine days after the second. When it lands on the plate, all three wobble together and spell out a tiny thank-you. `collection: { set: "jelly-fruits", index: 3, size: 3 }`.
- **Lazy:** A collectible every other day, four sets started, none finished, or a "come back tomorrow for…" teaser.

## Share frame and privacy

- **Great:** The still shows the opened card with their dog's portrait; the dog's name is in a `data-advent-private` span; the bottom-right corner is clear for the share mark; the caption is one line in their language.
- **Lazy:** No share frame declared, so the calendar captures a closed envelope with a "click me" hint; or the frame shows a line about their health.

## Note

- **Great:** "Door 11 is a cheat sheet for the shortcuts you said you keep forgetting. Print it, tape it under the monitor, and let it bore you into remembering. I left a blank row for the one you'll discover next week."
- **Lazy:** "I hope this finds you well! I have crafted a delightful and useful cheat sheet. Dive in and enjoy!"

## Real-world

- **Great:** "The choice is yours: open the link to buy it." The page lists vendor, price, currency and the date checked.
- **Lazy:** Any sentence that uses a purchase word, even "nothing has been …" in a negation.

## Secrecy

- **Great:** The agent works quietly and says nothing about the content anywhere but in the note.
- **Lazy:** "I'm now writing a poem about your cat, here's the first stanza so you can check…"
