# Concierge: great vs lazy

## Product
- **Great:** One exact item (name, colour, size left open with a size-guide link), wrapped in paper they tear open on screen; vendor page opened today, price 34.90 CHF with `checkedAt`, Christmas delivery cutoff, a fallback if it sells out, "Open the link, choose your size, pay. The choice is yours.", and a two-sentence reason tied to a shared hobby.
- **Lazy:** "A nice scarf from any shop, around 30 bucks", no link, no date, or worse, a note saying the scarf arrives Thursday.

## Book
- **Great:** The exact edition in their language, ISBN visible on the page, price and currency, why this book and not the obvious one, a library alternative, "Prepared for you. Buying it is yours to decide."
- **Lazy:** A bestseller from memory with a dead link and a price from two years ago.

## Experience
- **Great:** A workshop with a real date in January as a ticket they tear off; availability seen on the page, price per person for one (no partner assumed), how to get there by public transport, cancellation terms, what's left: "Pick the date, pay, add it to your calendar."
- **Lazy:** "Go see a show sometime!" or a line claiming two seats are already held for them.

## Hut night
- **Great:** A specific mountain hut with its official page, the room type, the price per night incl. half board, the dates the page shows as open, the approach and its difficulty, gear notes, a ceiling check, "Booking is yours to decide."
- **Lazy:** A hut that is closed in winter, no price, and a note pretending the bed is already sorted.

## Print services
- **Great:** For a sticker sheet: three real services that ship to Switzerland, each with the product page, price in CHF for one sheet, today's date and the matching PDF with 3 mm bleed; "Prepared for you. Printing is yours to decide."
- **Lazy:** "Print these at any shop", or one service that only ships within the US, with no price.
